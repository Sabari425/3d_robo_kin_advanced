This panel supplements the existing 3D, WS, Joints, Dyn, Code and Calib stages; it does not replace their controls.

    
Robot classification
Drive: electric (servomotors/steppers), hydraulic (high force and speed; cleanliness drawback), pneumatic (often tools/end effectors; compliant grasping).
Geometry: PPP Cartesian, RPP cylindrical, RRP spherical, SCARA, RRR articulated, Delta parallel.
Motion: point-to-point reaches programmed points; continuous-path control follows a prescribed path.

    
Specifications & workspace
DOF / axes: independent joint variables. Reachable workspace: all reachable TCP positions. Dexterous workspace: points reachable with arbitrary tool orientation; it is a subset of reachable workspace.
Gross work envelope: volume swept by the wrist. The first three major axes mainly position the wrist; minor wrist axes set tool orientation.
The existing simulator's workspace is a sampled approximation; it does not claim to compute dexterous workspace.

    
Accuracy, precision and repeatability
  • Accuracy: closeness to the commanded/true target.
  • Precision: closeness of repeated measurements to one another.
  • Repeatability: ability to return to the same pose under the same conditions.
Repeated points can cluster tightly away from the target: repeatable but inaccurate.

    
Standard DH convention
Each link transform is Aᵢ = Rz(θᵢ) Tz(dᵢ) Tx(aᵢ) Rx(αᵢ). The notes call the joint offset bᵢ; the simulator's existing table uses dᵢ. Here dᵢ (= bᵢ in the lecture notation).
Frame assignment: z along the joint axis; x along the common normal; y follows the right-hand rule. Modified DH is mentioned for comparison only; the live model remains standard DH.

    
Dynamics & actuation concepts
Newton–Euler recursion: forward pass propagates angular/linear velocities and accelerations; backward pass propagates forces and moments to obtain joint efforts. Gravity can be included by setting base acceleration a₀ = −g once.
Gear reduction trades speed for torque and can reduce reflected motor-side nonlinearities, but introduces friction, elasticity and backlash. Direct drive removes gear backlash but retains configuration-dependent coupling.

   

   

    
Mechanical gripper force
For n friction contacts, require n μ Fg ≥ SF · gf · m · g₀. Fg is the normal force per finger.

    
Vacuum cup
Ideal lifting force: F = ΔP · Aeff. Apply the safety factor to the required load.

    
Gripper selection
Physical constriction: finger shape geometrically supports the part. Friction constraint: friction resists gravity and acceleration; slip is the main risk.
External grip: fingers close around outside. Internal grip: fingers expand inside a feature. Double gripper: two independently controlled grippers can load and unload in one cycle.
Vacuum: best on flat, smooth, clean surfaces. Magnetic: fast pickup and tolerates holes, but residual magnetism and multiple-sheet pickup are drawbacks.

    
Linkage static analysis
Use moment balance about the finger pivot: ΣMₚ = 0. The lecture deck contains a geometry-dependent worked example (F ≈ 74.4 N, actuator ≈ 105.2 N), but its dimensions/angles are embedded in a slide figure and are not sufficiently recoverable as text here.
The benchmark is listed in Verification as a source-provided reference only; no geometry is invented to reproduce it.

   

   

    
2R planar forward kinematics
x = a₁ cosθ₁ + a₂ cos(θ₁+θ₂); y = a₁ sinθ₁ + a₂ sin(θ₁+θ₂); φ = θ₁+θ₂.

    
2D point transform
⁰p = ⁰o₁ + ⁰R₁ · ¹p. Example: rotate 90° and translate origin by [1, 2]ᵀ.
Rz(90°) [0,1]ᵀ = [−1,0]ᵀ
o = [1,2]ᵀ
⁰p = [0,2]ᵀ
The acceptance example's input point is not specified in the prompt; its stated expected result [1,3]ᵀ corresponds to a point [1,0]ᵀ under this rotation and translation.

    
Rotation matrix properties
A valid rotation matrix satisfies RᵀR = I and det(R) = +1. It contains nine entries but only three independent rotational degrees of freedom. Rotations do not commute: generally Rx(α)Ry(β) ≠ Ry(β)Rx(α).
Euler ZYZ: singular when sin θ = 0 (θ = 0 or π). RPY depends on whether rotations are interpreted about fixed or current axes; state that convention when converting.

    
Homogeneous transformation
T = [ R  p ; 0  1 ]. Its inverse is T⁻¹ = [ Rᵀ  −Rᵀp ; 0  1 ]. Chain transforms multiply in frame order: ⁰Tₙ = ⁰T₁ ¹T₂ … ⁿ⁻¹Tₙ.
At the TCP, n, o, a are the three orientation unit vectors (normal, orientation, approach) used by the lecture notation.

   

   

    
Cubic rest-to-rest trajectory
q(t)=q₀+Δq(3u²−2u³), q̇=Δq(6u−6u²)/T, q̈=Δq(6−12u)/T²; u=t/T.

    
Path versus trajectory
Path is the geometric locus in joint or operational space. Trajectory adds timing to position, velocity and acceleration.
Joint-space: convenient through via points and avoids direct Cartesian path constraints, but tool path is generally curved. Cartesian-space: can track a straight tool path, but needs IK at samples and may encounter unreachable poses or singularities.

    
Quintic rest-to-rest
For zero endpoint velocity and acceleration, use q=q₀+Δq(10u³−15u⁴+6u⁵). This gives q̇=q̈=0 at u=0 and u=1. General boundary velocities/accelerations require solving the six polynomial coefficients from six boundary conditions.

    
LSPB and feasibility
Linear segments with parabolic blends use constant acceleration in blend regions and constant velocity in the middle. For symmetric rest-to-rest motion, a = Δq/[tᵦ(T−tᵦ)] (as given in the prompt). Check 0 < tᵦ ≤ T/2. Then verify joint position, velocity, acceleration, jerk, collision and singularity limits along the full sampled path.

   

   

    
PD set-point tuning
For Jq̈+Bq̇=u−d, characteristic polynomial: Js²+(B+K_d)s+K_p. Select Kp=Jωₙ² and Kd=2ζωₙJ−B.

    
PID Routh–Hurwitz bound
For Js³+(B+Kd)s²+Kp s+Ki, stability requires positive coefficients and Ki < (B+Kd)Kp/J.

    
DC motor & drive concepts
L di/dt + Ri = V − Kbω; τ = Kt i. Under the electrical-time-constant approximation, the joint plant can be written Jq̈+Bq̇=u−d, with B = Bm + KbKt/R.
Decentralised: joints are controlled approximately independently, suitable under modest speed/acceleration and high gear reduction. Centralised: uses coupled manipulator dynamics for large speeds/accelerations. Computed torque: τ=M(q)[q̈d+Kdė+Kpe]+C(q,q̇)q̇+g(q).

    
Control methods
PD with gravity compensation adds the model gravity term to PD feedback. Inverse-dynamics/computed-torque control compensates the model's nonlinear dynamics. Feed-forward anticipates reference motion; feedback corrects tracking error and disturbances.
Robust and adaptive control are concept panels here; the supplied lecture gives topic coverage but not a complete design equation set. This lab does not claim to simulate full closed-loop manipulator control.

   

   

    
Contact and force control
Position control alone is insufficient for assembly, grinding and deburring where the robot contacts the environment. Force sensors provide interaction feedback.
Natural constraints come from physical environment geometry/contact. Artificial constraints are specified by the task strategy or program.
Examples: peg-in-hole, window washing/surface following and crank turning require controlling selected motion and force directions.

    
Twist, wrench and power
Twist ξ=[v;ω] and wrench F=[f;n]. Instantaneous power P=ξᵀF=vᵀf+ωᵀn. For reciprocal constraint pairs, ξᵀF=0.
Linear and angular components must use consistent frames and units. This panel explains the relationship; it does not model physical contact forces in the 3D renderer.

    
Impedance
Mechanical impedance relates force to velocity. A mass–damper–spring model has Z(s)=Ms+B+K/s. Inertial, resistive and capacitive terms dominate at different frequencies.
Hybrid impedance control coordinates motion and force objectives in different task directions. Real implementations are limited by sensor noise, impact dynamics, actuator saturation and environment uncertainty.

    
Force sensing
  • Wrist force/torque sensor: measures tool interaction wrench.
  • Joint torque sensor: estimates effort at joints.
  • Tactile/hand sensor: measures local contact distribution.
